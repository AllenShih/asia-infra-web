# Waratah Super Battery begins partial operation with 350 MW online

The initial 350 MW / 700 MWh entered service as part of an 850 MW / 1,680 MWh system designed to support the New South Wales grid.

EnergyCo announced on 5 August 2025 that the Waratah Super Battery had begun partial operation, with approximately half of the project's planned capacity online.

## What happened

The first 350 MW / 700 MWh of battery capacity entered service alongside the overarching control system, generator agreements and the first stage of associated network upgrades. EnergyCo described the project as a grid "shock absorber" intended to respond to major transmission outages.

## Key facts

- **Project:** Waratah Super Battery
- **Location:** Munmorah, New South Wales, Australia
- **Sector:** BESS
- **Capacity online at announcement:** 350 MW / 700 MWh
- **Planned total battery capacity:** 850 MW / 1,680 MWh
- **Battery operator:** Akaysha Energy
- **Network operator:** Transgrid
- **Event category:** Construction

## Why it matters

The milestone moved a major system-integrity project from construction into staged service. Its role differs from a conventional energy-arbitrage battery because the broader project combines storage, controls, paired generation and network upgrades to protect the NSW grid.

## Project and company context

The August 2025 release expected the remaining capacity later that year. EnergyCo's subsequently updated project page says testing and commissioning continued, with full operation expected during 2026. The partial-service milestone must therefore not be described as full COD.
